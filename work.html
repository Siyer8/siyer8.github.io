01 / Research

Open-Circuit Wind Tunnel

Designed, fabricated, and instrumented a compact wind tunnel for airflow testing.

Context

Undergraduate research at WPI with Prof. Mehdi Mortazavi, focused on building and instrumenting an open-circuit wind tunnel for airflow testing.

My role

I developed the tunnel geometry and modular CAD assembly, built the fan-control test rig, and carried out airflow measurements.

Exploded view

Scroll to separate the modular CAD assembly.

Contraction cone Test section · ATS probe Diffuser PWM fan + tachometer

What I built & how

I translated pressure and velocity requirements into a flow path, fabricated the tunnel, and used PWM control with interrupt-driven tachometer measurements to characterize fan RPM response.

Why this approach

The test rig connects fan commands to measured RPM; the ATS thermal-anemometer measurements then check the airflow the assembled tunnel actually produces. This makes physical measurements part of the design process.

Outcome & evidence

The completed tunnel produced a measured air velocity of 14.8 m/s. The gallery pairs CAD with the physical build and measurement evidence. The reconstructed graph is illustrative; the original measurement photograph is linked below.

02 / Machining

Single-Piston Engine

From steel and aluminum stock to a functional engine, with hands-on machining and CAM verification.

Context

A hands-on machining project completed in March–May 2025, turning steel and aluminum stock into a functional single-piston engine.

My role

I machined the components and assembled the engine, using a CNC, lathe, and drill press. I also created and verified machining toolpaths in Mastercam.

Exploded view

Scroll to take the engine apart, part by part.

Base Crankshaft Connecting rod Piston Cylinder

What I built & how

The work moved from individual parts to a complete assembly. Mastercam simulations provided a way to inspect cutting operations and potential collisions; the photographed base and completed engine show the fabrication work.

Why these details matter

Mating parts need to fit together for the mechanism to function. Working through both machining and assembly connected individual part geometry to the behavior of the complete engine.

Outcome & evidence

Completed a functional single-piston engine. The gallery shows the finished assembly, a machined component, and CAM planning. No speed, power, or efficiency claim is made without a measured test result.

03 / Software

AI Engineering Assistant

A locally run workspace for technical questions, project notes, and CAD/code workflows.

Context

A local software project developed to support engineering questions, project notes, and CAD/code workflows, with hardware integration for sensor queries.

My role

I developed the assistant in Python and Streamlit, integrating Ollama, ChromaDB, and Arduino serial communication.

What I built & how

The workspace supports local model selection, document retrieval, persistent project notes, chat history, and routing between project, advice, and CAD/code modes. Arduino integration enables real-time sensor tool calls.

Why this architecture

Local inference allows models to run on the device. Document retrieval brings engineering reference material into the workflow, while serial integration gives the assistant a path to physical sensor readings.

Outcome & evidence

Built a locally run assistant. The screenshots show the workspace and a CAD/code interaction; they document the interface, rather than establish a benchmark for model accuracy or hardware reliability.

04 / Concept design

FSAE Steering Wheel

An early concept translating driver-interface and packaging requirements into a CAD assembly.

Context

A May 2026 steering-wheel concept for the WPI Formula SAE car, exploring how driver controls and a display could fit within the cockpit.

My role

I developed the CAD concept in Onshape and evaluated control placement, grip geometry, driver visibility, and rear packaging.

Constraints & process

The concept explored a 6 × 11-inch package with a display, four buttons, two dials, paddle shifters, and a commercial quick release. The requirements image and assembly show how these elements were considered together.

Why these choices matter

Controls must be reachable while the display remains visible, and the rear components must fit the available space. Looking at the full assembly helps expose conflicts between driver interaction and packaging.

Outcome & next step

Produced an early CAD concept for potential vehicle integration. It is not presented as an installed or track-tested component. Physical fit and driver evaluation would be needed before progressing to a vehicle-ready design.

06 / Robotics

FRC NEO Motor Gearbox

A compact dual-motor gearbox assembly modeled for a FIRST Robotics Competition mechanism.

Context

A dual-NEO-motor gearbox CAD assembly for a FIRST Robotics Competition mechanism, developed within a team robotics setting.

My role

My contribution shown here is the Onshape assembly: modeling and arranging motors, gears, shafts, spacers, and support plates.

What I did & how

I organized the multi-stage gear train between parallel plates and inspected the complete assembly from perspective, top, and side views.

Why this layout

The parallel-plate arrangement keeps the transmission compact and makes the relationships between the motors, gears, shafts, and supports easier to inspect in CAD.

Outcome & evidence

Completed the documented CAD assembly. The images show packaging and component relationships; they do not establish a tested gear ratio, output torque, or competition performance.

07 / Product design

Split Ergonomic Keyboard

A custom split-keyboard case developed through iterative CAD and 3D-printed prototypes.

Context

A custom split-keyboard case project developed in January–March 2025, centered on component fit and individual hand geometry.

My role

I modeled the case in Fusion 360 and iterated the enclosure through multiple 3D-printed versions before assembling the keyboard halves.

What I built & how

I designed around the PCB and key layout, then used physical case iterations to refine component fit and wrist support. The photographs show printed shells and assembled prototypes.

Why prototype physically

CAD defines the geometry, but a printed enclosure makes component fit and the relationship to the user’s hands tangible. Typing comfort was a design objective rather than a quantified ergonomic result.

Outcome & evidence

Produced assembled split-keyboard prototypes and multiple printed case iterations. The physical builds demonstrate the progression from enclosure design to assembly.
